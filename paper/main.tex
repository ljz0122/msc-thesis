\section{Introduction}

Quasars are one of natures wonders. They are the electromagnetic manifestation of supermassive black holes (with masses upwards of one billion solar masses) that are in an "active" state. This means that they are accreting mass at a rate of a few solar masses per year and thereby emitting light at a flux equivalent to hundreds of galaxies. Quasars were first discovered in 1963 as radio sources. Already in 1965 it was discovered that there are 10 times more objects similar to quasars, but without strong radio emission (the called quasi-stellar galaxies - today we call them all quasars). Since then, it has been clear that selection effects play a major role for the study of quasars: the quasars in a given sample is highly dependent on how the sample was selected. In this thesis we will work on a new quasar selection technique that is supposed to be as "simply biased" as possible, namely based on astrometry. We will simply select an object to be a candidate quasar if the astrometry satellite Gaia sees the source as a compact source with no significant parallax and proper motion. In 2025 a large survey called PAQS (Purely Astrometric Quasar Survey) will commence and if we are lucky we may use some of that data in the thesis. As a start we will use data from a stepping stone pre-survey called SNAQS (Small NOT Astrometric Quasar Survey) where we will analyze a much smaller sample selected to a brighter flux limit. We will use the SNAQS sample to make some forecasts of what PAQS may find in terms of stellar contamination, galaxy contamination, quasar redshift distribution, distribution of quasar properties (dust extinction, broad absorption lines, etc.). In particular, we will begin this MSc project by working on the observations of quasar candidates from SNAQS. And hopefully, we may also contain some new data from PAQS which will begin in the autumn of 2025. It could be an opportunity to verify the forecasts we get form data of SNAQS. This project will involve reducing raw data (extraction, wavelength calibration, and flux calibration) and analyzing the reduced spectra (classification, redshift determination).
