\begin{abstract}

    The Smaller NOT Astrometric Quasar Survey (SNAQS) aims to construct a $100\%$ complete sample of quasars with $m_G < 19$ within a 245-square-degree region around the North Galactic Pole. It serves as a pilot survey for the more extensive Purely Astrometric Quasar Survey (PAQS) planned for the southern sky. Building upon the purely astrometric selection methods introduced by \citet{heintz_study_2015}—which established that approximately $80\%$ of stationary sources near the galactic poles are quasars—we analyzed a new set of spectra from SNAQS. In this work, we determined the actual fraction of quasars among these stationary sources and identified missing populations by comparing our results with existing spectroscopic surveys. Based on these findings, we project the expected yields for PAQS. Finally, we leveraged recent Gaia Data Release 3 to propose a more efficient and accurate methodology for selecting astrometrically stationary objects as quasar candidates.

    \vspace{1em}
    \noindent \textbf{Keywords:} Quasar, Survey, Astrometry, Gaia, Spectrum
    
\end{abstract}

\chapter{Conclusion}

We have almost finished a prior survey for PAQS as SNAQS. In conclusion, our results illustrate 63 of 88 observed objects, or $\sim72\%$, are quasars. After combination with other large survey results, $\sim87\%$ targets in SNAQS selection are quasars. This is in good accordance with previous results from \citet{heintz_study_2015}. Based on this ratio, $\sim 38,600$ targets will not be identified as quasars in PAQS which contains $\sim315,000$ targets. This ratio could be lower because we higher estimated the number of quasars combination with other large survey results and it will cover a bigger area (from the pole down to about \qty{\sim30}{\degree}) in PAQS, while SNAQS just covered a region up to less than \qty{10}{\degree} from the galactic north.

At the same time, we introduced a potential new method could push the ratio to $\sim96.5\%$ ($\sim2\sigma$). While the new selection still contains a number of sources without existing spectra and the classification results of large survey are also lack of visual verification. These need some following research.
